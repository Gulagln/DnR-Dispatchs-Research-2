Minutes released Wednesday from the Federal Reserve's most recent policy meeting showed a wider-than-usual split among committee members over how quickly to keep cutting rates into year-end.

Several participants argued that with the labor market cooling gradually rather than cracking, there was little urgency to move again before more data arrived. Others pushed back, pointing to early signs of softening in hiring intentions and arguing that waiting too long risked being behind the curve.

The sticking point, according to the minutes, was shelter inflation. A few officials flagged it as the main reason core price growth hasn't cooled as fast as the rest of the basket, and said they wanted more confirmation it was actually rolling over before committing to another cut.

**Why it matters:** Futures markets had been pricing a cut at the next meeting as close to a coin flip. Nothing in the minutes moves that needle decisively — if anything, it confirms the committee itself is genuinely split, which means incoming data between now and the meeting will do more of the work than the Fed's own language.
