The core Personal Consumption Expenditures index, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, held roughly flat month-over-month for a third straight reading, according to data out Thursday.

Goods prices continued to drift lower, extending a trend that's been in place most of the year. Services inflation was the offset again — stickier, and doing most of the work keeping the headline number from cooling further.

Economists were split on how to read it. One camp called it confirmation that the last leg of disinflation is the hardest, and that the Fed has room to be patient. The other read the flat print as evidence the easy progress is over and further declines will take longer than the market currently expects.

**Why it matters:** This was the last major inflation print before the next FOMC meeting. It didn't hand either side of the committee's internal debate a clean win, which means the meeting itself is likely to be closer than usual.
